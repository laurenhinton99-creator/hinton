The Room Where Nobody Goes

July 31, 2026

July is slow for work in New York. Most people are traveling or remote from whatever summer home is inviting them back in. This July was different in that work was not so slow. There were things to do and people to meet and it felt like the summer lull quietly skipped over our office off Lafayette street. The partners were away doing what one does when they’ve earned the title of Partner, but the rest of the well-oiled machine kept on chugging.

Our firm was between funds in July awaiting a first-close and there was still a lot to do. Continuing on with the idea of “what does Platform mean,” I had determined that a part of the meaning had to do with bringing in people our firm has not previously had a relationship with before. Our “net-new network” that could act as Advisers, Design Partners, etc. We talked about these new network folks as potential “superconnectors” or people that we believed would be helpful for our founders at some point in their journey. We decided to start with CISOs and have since moved to Heads of Product and more recently CFOs. I have never been a “Head of” or “C of” of anything before, so tapping my own network to find these people was not the place to start. I also quickly discovered that a lot of these people tend to not want to be found. They have their own lives, own incentives, and are incredibly protective of their time - a skill I have yet to master.

I, on the other hand, still have a lot of time.

So much so that I thought a lot about where to find certain CFOs for our founders and my thoughts guided me to think about where CFOs gather. What rooms would they be interested in being in? I started researching and came across a CFO panel where the topic of discussion was: My Worst Day as a CFO. I, being someone who has never been a CFO, was immediately curious about the worst day in the position. I signed up to attend and was thankfully allowed on the list.

Quickly noting here that New York is full of events and evening obligations, something I love and dislike about the city. Sometimes you sign up for things and can’t will yourself to go to them even when you know you should. Not being a CFO, I’ll admit that this was one of those evenings. It was also pouring rain and the thought of traveling to midtown with no umbrella was extremely unappealing. Reluctantly, I convinced myself to go and made a self-promise that I would meet at least three new people before giving into the weather and making my way back to the west side.

I hadn’t been to a panel or event hosted by this group before but I wasn’t surprised that the room mainly consisted of older men. I had learned that a lot of people with experience in the sectors I was interested in happened to be older men. This was intimidating at first. Speaking to someone who could list a portion of their career in the same timeline that I could state the years I’d been alive. I still go back and forth between feeling like an imposter and feeling like “who cares.” People only care about the last thing you’ve done, and through that lens I choose to think we’re all on equal footing. Nevertheless, my confidence is still being built up and it doesn’t help that I look young. Anywho…

Once the panel started, I glanced at the titles on the screen and noticed one CFO was working for a prominent consumer VC in New York. I found that to be a good sign that I decided to show up. Each CFO spoke their piece and I was genuinely impressed with the stories each one told. The room was small, but each CFO spoke candidly and there was an understanding that the evening was meant for honesty and asking good questions. I managed to sneak a question in and felt the room perk up a little. I had that inner feeling where you sense that there was some respect received just due to you asking a good question - a skill I am closer to mastering.

I met some interesting people and, specifically, got to talk to one of the CFOs and the host for the evening, both of which were connected to the CFO network in New York. I left smiling to myself and relieved that the evening was worthwhile. On the subway home I couldn’t help but think that I did not know anyone in that room when I walked in, a rare occurrence for New York venture as there is bound to be somebody somewhere that you have chatted with before. I really liked the idea that there wasn’t.

You hear the phrase “having a seat at the table” a lot in business and I can confidently say I’ve been eager more times than not to be “in the room” or amongst the people calling the shots. The train back downtown made me wonder if sometimes it is best to be in the rooms where people are not. To find the places where people haven’t looked yet. I just couldn’t help myself in making the comparison that being in the room where no one else was is similar to a founder building for a world no one has thought of.

The rain sucked and I know I won’t always get accepted into these rooms as my purpose for being there is not always clear. Despite this, I have started looking out for the places that are not obviously desirable, but still have a purpose. It’s sometimes difficult to decide whether a room is less frequented because no one knows about it or because its quality is not up to par. Still figuring this out, but I’m excited to meet the people I find when the room just so happens to fall under the former.

Cheers to July.
