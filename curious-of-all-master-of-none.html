Curious of All, Master of None

September 30, 2026

My partner this week told me he thinks writing is therapeutic for me. Not a groundbreaking statement, but it struck me that I never thought about it that way. It is WAY easier to write down how I feel than to voice it to a stranger. Shoutout google docs and Publii.

This month I’m expanding on August’s edition with the idea of “branding” and “personal opinions that you share externally for others to critique on.” I’m speaking to a friend of mine who is a quasi-influencer in Chicago, mainly in the food scene, to hear her tips and tricks for introducing herself to the world.

In preparation for that call, I’ve been ping-ponging back and forth in my mind on what makes the most sense for someone in my position (age, career, location, all of the things). Do you optimize for broader, generic, reach when thinking about content you share, or do you go the exact opposite way and focus on one niche that could potentially set you apart. I’m most curious to hear my friend’s take on this but my answer changes every day. On the one hand, does anyone really care to watch another “Day in the life”? Likely not. The obsession with the mundane can reach more people but I’m not convinced my “mundane” is what people care about.

Going more from the niche community perspective seems like a way to build a relatable audience, but that leads to the next unknown of what that niche actually is. If I think about it for too long, I internally cower and silently, definitively, claim that there is nothing worth talking about that people don’t already find on their own. I then start to spiral as well and ask questions about “do I need to become an expert in x” or “should I play into a bit that associates me with y” and there are almost too many options.

So where the hell do you start?

And how the hell do you start?

GENUINELY!

People offer the common saying of “you just have to get through cringe mountain.” Well, I’m not even at base camp. I cringe at the thought of me cringing. It’s such a mental block that I go into a paralysis and end up kicking myself as I’ve talked myself out of taking action once again. What do you let people see? How honest do you get? Does anyone even care? These questions plague me but I just can’t shake the feeling that I need to do something about it.

When I actually think of what people enjoy and relate to when it comes to myself I think about the questions I ask them. The pure inquisitiveness I bring to the table because I actually enjoy uncovering the answers. It feels so SOFT. It bothers me that there isn’t a quantitative measure to this or that I can’t think of an area where me being a “thought leader” (gross, still hate that phrase) comes into play. To lean on my experience alone feels fraudulent as well because I haven’t done anything yet!

I’m rambling now, but you see my dilemma.

But really, DO I need to do anything? Is talking about myself or putting myself out there being humble while simultaneously bragging. I go back and forth on whether this feeling, this internal push, is something that is a result of the environment we live in now, almost instinctual as a means for survival, or something narcissistically driven. It all goes back to: does anyone really care or is it up to me to make someone care?

Even this blog is a prime example. I designed, coded, and purchased a domain for it and am loyal to keeping it updated monthly and, yet, I don’t promote it or share it. I have no way of knowing who reads it. It’s a push and a pull of sorts on what I want to do and what I actually do.

It seems this month I have a lot more questions than I have answers.
